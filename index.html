Inside the brain and the heart

Pull each one apart to see its working parts. Tap a part, or its name in the list, to read what it does.

The brain

Loading the 3D model

  • Planning, decision making and self-control. It also drives voluntary movement and a large part of your personality.

  • Brings together touch, temperature and pain, and keeps track of where your body is in space.

  • Hearing and understanding language, and building memories together with the hippocampus tucked inside them.

  • Vision. It receives the signals from your eyes and turns them into what you see.

  • Balance, coordination, and smooth, practised movement.

  • Keeps you alive without thinking: breathing, heart rate, and the sleep and wake cycle. It also links the brain to the spinal cord.

  • Turns what you experience into new long-term memories.

  • Spots threats and drives fear and other strong emotions. It also helps attach emotion to memories.

The heart

Loading the 3D model

  • Receives oxygen-poor blood coming back from the body through the vena cava.

  • Pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.

  • Receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.

  • The strongest chamber. It pumps oxygen-rich blood out to the whole body through the aorta.

  • The body's largest artery. It carries oxygen-rich blood from the heart to the rest of the body.

  • Carries oxygen-poor blood from the heart to the lungs. It is one of the few arteries that carries oxygen-poor blood.

  • The two largest veins, one from the upper body and one from the lower. They bring oxygen-poor blood back to the right atrium.

  • Carry oxygen-rich blood from the lungs to the left atrium. They are among the few veins that carry oxygen-rich blood.

  • Wrap around the outside of the heart and feed the heart muscle itself with oxygen-rich blood. If one is blocked, part of the muscle is starved of oxygen, which is what causes a heart attack.

  • Collect the used, oxygen-poor blood from the heart muscle and drain it into the right atrium, mostly through a large vein called the coronary sinus.

  • Four one-way doors, between the atria and ventricles and at the exits of the heart, that stop blood flowing backwards. Their closing makes the sound of a heartbeat.

A model for learning, based on one person's scans, so real brains and hearts vary. In the heart, blue vessels carry oxygen-poor blood and red vessels carry oxygen-rich blood. The amygdala is a simplified shape. If the 3D view does not load, the list still tells you what every part does.
