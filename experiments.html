Experiments

We believe in learning by doing. Every experiment must have measurable outcomes, and we document what worked, what didn't, and why.

Where we are

We have not run an experiment yet. These are the first two we are planning. When one runs, its method and results, including anything that fails, will be published here.

StrokeSense

Concept, not built or tested yet

A purpose-built, dry-electrode EEG headband that detects stroke by measuring electrical asymmetry between the brain's two hemispheres directly, the actual physiological event of a stroke, instead of relying on external, easily-mimicked symptoms like facial drooping or slurred speech.

How we would frame it: a screening and early-warning prototype, checked against existing published stroke-EEG datasets, not a certified medical diagnostic device. Every alert would be followed by a call to emergency services.

AirNudge

Concept, not built or tested yet

A wearable sensor-and-actuator system that detects an obstructive sleep apnea event as it happens and delivers a gentle vibration to prompt a position change, reopening the airway without needing a face mask or a machine.

How we would frame it: a positional-therapy aid and research prototype, a supplement to, not a replacement for, proper medical diagnosis through a sleep study.

How we run experiments

  • Written parental or guardian consent for every volunteer before anything is recorded.
  • A teacher or adviser with a science background reviews the plan first. For anything electronic, someone with electronics or physics knowledge checks it before it touches a person.
  • We never simulate anything medically risky. Dangerous or rare conditions are studied with existing open, anonymised research data, and live tests only use safe, voluntary, reversible actions.
  • Sessions are short and supervised, and anyone can opt out at any time.
  • We never give medical advice or run health interventions.
