Neurology

Understanding your brain, nervous system, and how your mind works

What is neurology?

Neurology is the branch of medicine that studies the nervous system—your brain, spinal cord, and nerves. It explores how these systems control everything you do: thinking, moving, feeling, and remembering.

The brain: your control center

Your brain contains about 86 billion neurons (brain cells) that communicate through electrical and chemical signals. It weighs only 3 pounds but controls your entire body and personality.

The nervous system network

Your nervous system is divided into two parts: the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and the peripheral nervous system (nerves throughout your body). Together, they form your body's communication network.

Key neurology concepts for beginners

Neurons

The basic building blocks of the nervous system. Each neuron communicates with others through synapses (connections), creating the electrical and chemical signals that power your thoughts and actions.

Neurotransmitters

Chemical messengers that allow neurons to communicate. Examples include dopamine (motivation), serotonin (mood), and acetylcholine (memory). Imbalances can affect mental health.

Neuroplasticity

Your brain's ability to reorganize and form new neural connections throughout life. This means you can learn new skills and recover from brain injuries—your brain is always changing!

Gray matter vs white matter

Gray matter contains neuron bodies and processes information. White matter contains axons (connections) and transmits signals. Both are essential for brain function.
