Cardiology

Understanding your heart, circulation, and cardiovascular health

What is cardiology?

Cardiology is the branch of medicine that studies the heart and circulatory system. Your heart is a powerful muscle that pumps blood throughout your body, delivering oxygen and nutrients to every cell.

The heart: your lifeline

Your heart beats about 100,000 times per day, pumping approximately 2,000 gallons of blood. It's about the size of your fist but powerful enough to sustain your entire body for a lifetime.

Blood vessels: the highway network

Your circulatory system includes arteries (carry blood away), veins (return blood), and capillaries (tiny vessels for exchange). Together, they form a network over 60,000 miles long—enough to wrap around Earth twice!

Key cardiology concepts for beginners

Arteries vs veins

Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart (usually bright red). Veins return oxygen-poor blood to the heart (usually dark red/blue). Both are essential for circulation.

Heart rate and pulse

Your heart rate is how many times your heart beats per minute (normal: 60-100 bpm at rest). Your pulse is the heartbeat you can feel in your wrists or neck.

Cholesterol and plaque

High cholesterol can build up as plaque in arteries, narrowing them and restricting blood flow. This increases heart disease risk. Regular exercise and healthy eating help maintain healthy cholesterol.

Arrhythmias

Irregular heartbeats can be harmless or serious. Stress, caffeine, or underlying conditions can cause them. Most are manageable with lifestyle changes or medical treatment.

Chest pain, fainting or a racing heart that will not settle?

Do not wait. Call 995 (or go to the nearest emergency department). The articles here are for learning, not for checking symptoms.
